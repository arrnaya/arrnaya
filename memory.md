## 🧠 Style & Voice Notes

*Lessons learned from previous generations. Apply to every new post.*

**What works well:**
- Opening with a provocative reframe of conventional wisdom
- Using institutional comparisons as the spine of technical posts
- Quantitative formulas formatted in monospaced code blocks give technical credibility
- Terminal-style pseudocode for protocol flows outperforms prose-only explanations
- Phase/step cards (numbered 1–5) work well for procedural content
- FAQ sections with native `<details>` elements outperform JS accordions for AI search indexing

**What to avoid:**
- Generic AI tells: "In conclusion...", "It is worth noting...", "It is important to..."
- Listing statistics without context or comparison
- Academic hedging ("some argue that", "it could be said")
- Bullet points as the primary content format — use prose with strategic tables
- Repeating the same aesthetic without evolution

**Voice calibration:**
- Write as a practitioner who has shipped production systems, not a commentator who has read about them
- Acknowledge failure modes and real risks — do not be promotional about any technology
- Use "we" sparingly; prefer "institutions", "protocol teams", "firms"
- Analogies should be from engineering, finance, or systems design — not pop culture
